Business Life: Preference & Compare–Contrast Lab

Group discussion guide — state a preference, compare alternatives, support an argument, and propose a relevant third option.

How the activity works

Your group will choose ONE of the five business dilemmas. Do not simply say which option is “better.” Compare both sides, justify your preference, acknowledge a weakness, and develop a relevant alternative.

1. Understand the dilemma
Identify the real decision a business would have to make.
2. Compare
Use clear criteria such as cost, people, risk, flexibility, growth, or long-term impact.
3. Argue
Give reasons and evidence for your preference, not just personal taste.
4. Extend
Offer a practical third option, compromise, or condition under which both sides could work.

B · Choose ONE Scenario

Every group may choose any one of the five. Select the scenario your group finds most interesting or discussable.

A. Profit vs. Social Impact

Situation: A profitable company wants to grow, but leadership also wants the business to create measurable social benefits.

Think about: investor expectations, employees, customers, reputation, long-term sustainability.

B. Startup vs. Established Corporation

Situation: A talented graduate receives two offers: join a fast-moving startup or work for a large established corporation.

Think about: stability, autonomy, learning, salary, promotion, risk.

C. Remote Work vs. Office Work

Situation: A company is deciding whether most employees should work remotely or return to the office.

Think about: productivity, communication, teamwork, flexibility, cost, company culture.

D. Fast Growth vs. Sustainable Growth

Situation: A successful company can expand quickly into new markets, but rapid expansion may increase financial and operational risk.

Think about: speed, quality, risk, investment, customer trust, long-term resilience.

E. Local Sourcing vs. Global Sourcing

Situation: A food company must decide whether to buy more ingredients locally or use international suppliers.

Think about: price, quality, reliability, local jobs, supply risk, flexibility.

C · Your Group's Discussion

Do not answer only from personal preference. Treat the scenario as a decision a real organization must make.

1. What is the central decision in this scenario?

2. Compare the two main options using TWO clear criteria.

3. What does your group prefer, and what are your TWO strongest reasons?

4. What is the strongest reasonable argument for the other side?

5. What is ONE weakness, risk, or limitation of your own preferred option?

6. Under what condition might the other option actually be better?

7. Propose ONE relevant third option, compromise, or hybrid solution.

8. Give your group's position in ONE strong sentence.
